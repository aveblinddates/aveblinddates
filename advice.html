DATING ADVICE

Dating is a skill
you can learn.

A first date doesn't have to be perfect. It just needs to give two people a chance to get to know each other.

BEFORE THE DATE

Start with the right mindset.

Dating can feel intimidating, especially when you're meeting someone for the first time. Instead of worrying about whether the other person will like you, focus on whether you enjoy getting to know them.

You don't need to figure out your entire relationship during one conversation. The goal of a first date is literally just to discover whether you'd like to spend more time together.

FIRST DATE BASICS

A few things to remember.

01

Be curious.

Ask questions because you're genuinely interested in the answer. Good conversations usually happen when both people are curious about each other.

Try asking about someone's favorite way to spend a free afternoon instead of relying only on standard small talk.
02

Listen.

Don't spend the entire date planning your next response. Pay attention to what the other person says and follow up on things that interest you.

Remembering something they mentioned earlier can make the conversation feel much more natural. Be normal. Please.
03

Be yourself.

You don't have to create a perfect version of yourself for a first date. Being honest gives the other person a real chance to get to know you. However if part of your personality involves not wearing deodarant, then you should change yourself.

You are not trying to impress everyone. You're trying to discover whether two people fit well together.
04

Put your phone away.

I love doomscrolling as much as the next person, but being on your phone during a date can make it a lot harder to actually connect. Give the person in front of you your attention.

Unless you're dealing with something important, consider keeping your phone out of sight during the date.
05

Don't rush.

One good conversation doesn't mean you need to decide immediately where the relationship is going. Let things develop naturally. A lot of you went to that relationship talk, remember first date ≠ marriage.

If you had a good time, a second date can simply be another opportunity to learn more.
06

Give people a chance.

First impressions matter, but they aren't always a true reflection of the other person. Someone might be nervous, quieter than usual, or simply different from what you expected.

Ask yourself whether you genuinely disliked something or whether it was simply unfamiliar.

CONVERSATION

Not sure what to talk about?

You don't need a list of scripted questions, in fact you probably shouldn't have one. But having a few ideas in your back pocket can help if the conversation gets quiet.

01

Favorites

Favorite music, movies, books, food, microwave brand, places, or things to do around campus.

02

Stories

Ask about a memorable trip, funny experience, crazy college stories so far, childhood memory, or something they've recently enjoyed.

03

Interests

Talk about hobbies, classes, activities, sports, music, or something they could spend hours learning about.

04

The future

If it feels natural, talk about goals, dreams, places you'd like to visit, or what you're looking forward to. BUT DO NOT ASK SOMEONE TO MARRY YOU ON A FIRST DATE!!!

LOOK FOR

What makes a good date?

There isn't a universal checklist for a successful relationship. But healthy dating generally involves mutual respect, honest communication, and interest from both people.

♡

You feel comfortable being yourself.

You don't feel like you have to constantly perform or pretend to be someone you're not. But again, please wear deodorant.

♡

Conversation goes both ways.

Both people have opportunities to talk, listen, ask questions, and share.

♡

Boundaries are respected.

Both people feel comfortable saying no, changing plans, or communicating what they're comfortable with.

♡

You leave wanting to know more.

You don't need to know everything after one date. Sometimes curiosity is enough reason for another conversation.

IF IT WENT WELL

Should you ask for a second date?

If you enjoyed spending time together and would like to see the person again, you don't need a complicated strategy. Be clear and respectful.

Something as simple as "I had a really good time. Would you want to do this again?" can be enough. But remember, if they say no, that is okay and you need to respect that.
